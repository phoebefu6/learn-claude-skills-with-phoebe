Part 0

One stack, retargeted per function

The aidm bucket - AI for a domain - is the largest in the hub, spanning data-analysis, data-science, management, marketing, media, design, HR, finance, education, sports, research, and attribution. It looks like twelve different toolkits. It isn't. It's one skill stack pointed at different work: a shelf of specialists you pull from, a multiplexer that turns one idea into every channel, a few domain experts, and a research front-end that reads once and serves everyone. This session teaches the retargeting move, and for each skill names what it does, when to reach for it, and its token effect.

Part 1 · the marketing-skills pack (~34k★)

The marketing-skills pack 7 min live

The marketing-skills pack is roughly 50 skills - and the mistake is treating it as one thing. It's a shelf: you pull the single skill that matches the task at hand and leave the rest on disk. content-strategy for a plan, copywriting for the words, seo-audit to fix a page, pricing to set a number. Because of progressive disclosure, the 49 you don't use cost you nothing.

LiveA shelf you pull one skill from2 min▶

The pack covers most of the marketing lifecycle. You don't run "marketing-skills"; you run the one that fits:

You're doing...Reach for
Planning the quarter's contentcontent-strategy

Fixing a page that won't rankseo-audit, ai-seo
Setting or testing a pricepricing, offers
Shipping a launch or adslaunch, ads, emails, cold-email

★ Try it nowI have a landing page that isn't converting. Use the seo-audit skill on this URL and give me the three highest-impact fixes ranked by effort: [paste URL or the page copy].
LiveWhy "shelf" is the right mental model2 min▶

Treating the pack as one giant skill would either bloat every task with 50 skills' worth of instruction, or force you to know which of 50 to name before you start. The shelf model fixes both: each skill has its own trigger, so the right one surfaces when your task matches, and only its body loads.

  • When - any marketing task, from strategy to a single email subject line.
  • Token effect - progressive disclosure means installing all 50 is nearly free; you pay only for the one that fires.
  • Habit - describe the task, let the matching skill surface, rather than memorizing the catalogue.
Don't name the pack, name the job Say what you're trying to do ("audit this page for SEO", "price this tier"). The right shelf skill fires on the job description - you don't need to have the catalogue memorized.
Self-studyThe full shelf at a glance1 min read▶

Beyond the headline skills, the pack reaches into: analytics, cro, ab-testing, onboarding, churn-prevention, referrals, pricing, offers, paywalls, ad-creative, product-marketing, public-relations, competitor-profiling, lead-magnets, programmatic-seo, schema, site-architecture, sms, social, and more. You will never use all of them in one week - and that's the point. The shelf is broad so that whatever function you're in, the specialist already exists and fires on demand.

Part 2 · the content engine

One idea, every channel 6 min live

The reuse move in its clearest form: research an idea once, publish it everywhere - each output fitted to its platform, not copy-pasted. content-multiplexer takes one topic and produces LinkedIn, Instagram, X, and a newsletter draft, each platform-native. The li-top-voice-engine and its day sub-skills run the same idea through a structured weekly cadence.

Livecontent-multiplexer - one topic, four drafts2 min▶

content-multiplexer extracts the single core idea from a topic, then writes for each platform in that platform's own voice, format, and length. A LinkedIn post is not an Instagram caption with the hashtags swapped - the multiplexer knows the difference.

  • When - you have one insight and want it live across your channels this week.
  • What you give it - a topic, optionally a chart, screenshot, or day-theme.
  • Token effect - the expensive part (finding and shaping the idea) happens once; the four drafts reuse it rather than re-researching four times.
★ Try it nowUse content-multiplexer on this one insight and give me a LinkedIn post, an Instagram carousel outline, an X thread, and a newsletter section - each native to its platform: [paste your insight].
Liveli-top-voice-engine and its day sub-skills2 min▶

The li-top-voice-engine is the master brain for a LinkedIn presence; it routes production to per-day sub-skills so each day has a repeatable format:

Same architecture as the Core Stack: one engine, specialist sub-skills that fire on their day. The engine holds strategy; the sub-skills hold format.

Self-studyWhy one research pass is the reuse token-move1 min read▶

The costly part of content isn't the writing - it's the finding, verifying, and shaping of the idea. If you research separately for each platform, you pay that cost four times over and risk four slightly different versions of the truth. Multiplexing pays it once and fans the result out. That's the "reuse it" move from Session 1 applied to content: stable, expensive work done once and reused across many cheap outputs.

Part 3 · specialists for specific functions

Domain specialists 6 min live

Beyond marketing, a handful of skills own a whole function each. phoebe-design-partner is the visual brain for any team's output, phoebe-data-schema-to-insights owns the analysis and DS domain, and resume-builder-harvard plus the proficiently pack cover HR, recruiting, and career.

Livephoebe-design-partner - visual work for any function2 min▶

phoebe-design-partner is the supervisor for every visual artifact - decks, dashboards, matrices, web pages, landing pages, posters. Whatever function you're in, when the output needs to look right, this is the skill, and it routes through the taste and anti-slop gate before anything ships.

  • When - any "design this", "make it look professional", "recolor", "build a screen".
  • Token effect - it encodes the design discipline once, so you don't re-explain palette, encoding, and layout rules on every visual task.
★ Try it nowUse phoebe-design-partner to turn this rough table into a clean, professional one-page matrix I could put in front of an exec: [paste the table].
Liveschema-to-insights and the career pack2 min▶
  • phoebe-data-schema-to-insights - the analysis/DS specialist: it goes from a raw schema to a data contract to an offline insight, so an analytics question isn't answered from a blank prompt but from a disciplined pipeline.
  • resume-builder-harvard - creates, reviews, and tailors resumes and CVs, including ATS optimization and tailoring to a specific job description.
  • proficiently pack - the wider HR/recruiting/career shelf: job search, tailoring, cover letters, network scanning.
Real world

A hiring manager and a candidate can use the same career pack from opposite ends - one to write a role-tailored JD-matched resume, the other to screen against it. Same skill shelf, two functions, retargeted.

Self-studyThe retargeting principle1 min read▶

Notice what's happening across Parts 1-3: the same small set of moves - a shelf of specialists, a multiplexer, a design gate, a research front-end - covers marketing, content, design, analysis, and HR. You don't learn a new toolkit per domain; you point the same toolkit at new work. That's why the aidm bucket has twelve courses but one architecture, and why learning the pattern once pays across every function you'll ever sit in.

Part 4 · the shared research front-end

Research once, apply everywhere 3 min live

Finance, education, research, and sports domains all share one need: a trustworthy read of a fast-moving question. deep-research is the shared front-end - a subagent fans out across sources, adversarially verifies claims, and returns a cited brief, so the reading happens off your main context and comes back compressed.

Livedeep-research as a shared front-end2 min▶

deep-research is the same skill whether the question is a finance thesis, a curriculum reference, an academic literature scan, or a sports-analytics query. It fans out searches, fetches and cross-checks sources, and returns a synthesized, cited report.

  • When - any multi-source question where you need to trust the answer and cite it.
  • How it stays cheap - a subagent does the reading in its own budget and hands back only the brief.
  • Token effect - it offloads the heavy reading and compresses the result, so your main context holds the conclusion, not the raw pages.
Ask a sharp question first deep-research pays off most when the question is specific. If it's fuzzy ("what should I know about X"), narrow it first - scope, timeframe, what decision it feeds - so the brief comes back usable, not sprawling.
Self-studyOffload + compress, together1 min read▶

deep-research is where two Session 1 moves stack: offload (a subagent with its own budget does the reading) and compress (it returns a brief, not the sources). That combination is why one deep-research call can answer a question that would blow your whole context window if you tried to read every source inline. The cited brief is small enough to then feed content-multiplexer, a design-partner deck, or a decision - the research done once, applied everywhere.

Offload plus compress: the subagent reads in its own budget and hands back the brief, so your main window holds the conclusion, not the raw pages.
