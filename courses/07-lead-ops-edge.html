Part 1 · lead track - decision quality

Think with a panel 6 min live

The most expensive leadership mistake is a confident decision made from one angle. Two skills widen the aperture: core-mentor-room gives you a roundtable of named mentors, each with a distinct lens, before you decide; deep-research pulls the facts you'd otherwise guess at - regulation deltas, an emerging-tech landscape - as a cited report.

Livecore-mentor-room - a roundtable before you commit3 min▶

core-mentor-room convenes a 22-person panel of named mentors - builders, CEOs, data leaders, and coaches - each speaking in character from their known positions. For any given topic you select the 5-8 most relevant, always spanning categories, so you get genuinely different lenses rather than one voice repeated.

  • When to reach for it - any real decision with trade-offs: an architecture call, a career move, a stakeholder problem, a team-morale issue.
  • What it gives you - consensus points and productive disagreements, then a concrete recommendation.
★ Try it nowConvene the mentor room on this decision: [your real decision]. Pick the 5-8 most relevant mentors, give me each distinct lens in two sentences, then the consensus, the disagreement, and one recommended next step.
Real world

Facing "do I take the bigger role or the better team," a panel of five surfaced the one factor the excitement was hiding - a two-year identity cost the strategy lens named and the calm lens confirmed. The decision changed because the angle did.

Livedeep-research - facts before opinions2 min▶

deep-research fans out across many sources, fetches them, verifies claims adversarially, and returns a cited synthesis. It's the offload move applied to research: a helper spends its own budget reading, and you get the conclusion.

  • When to reach for it - gov regulation deltas ("what changed in the EU AI Act this quarter"), an emerging-tech landscape (crypto, web3, quantum) you need to understand, not just skim.
  • Before you invoke - if the question is fuzzy, it asks 2-3 clarifiers first, so the report answers what you actually need.

Part 3 · prod track - the biggest ops token-saver

Automate the workflow itself 6 min live

Here is the highest-leverage move in the whole course. A behavior you re-prompt every time costs tokens every time. A behavior you configure once - a hook, a schedule, an allowlist - fires automatically and costs nothing to re-request. This is automation applied to your own workflow, not just your tasks.

Liveupdate-config - behaviors that fire automatically ⚡2 min▶

update-config edits your settings.json to make the harness do something automatically - a hook. The key insight: an automated behavior ("whenever I finish a task, run the linter") can't live in memory or a preference, because Claude doesn't run those - the harness runs hooks. So a recurring instruction becomes configuration instead of a sentence you repeat.

  • When to reach for it - any "from now on, whenever X, do Y" request; permissions; env vars.
  • Token effect ⚡ - the behavior stops being re-requested every session. Configure once, save forever.
Real world

A team kept typing "convert uploaded files to markdown first." Turned into a PostToolUse hook, it now just happens on every upload - the instruction went from a per-session sentence to zero-cost configuration.

Liveschedule & loop - recurring runs2 min▶
  • schedule - create recurring cloud agents (routines) on a cron schedule, or a one-time future run. Reach for it when a task should happen at a time whether or not you're there - a daily digest, a Monday report.
  • loop - run a prompt or slash command on an interval within a session ("check the deploy every 5 minutes"). For polling and repeated checks, not one-offs.

Both remove the "remind me to run this again" overhead - the work initiates itself.

Livefewer-permission-prompts - cut the round-trips ⚡2 min▶

fewer-permission-prompts scans your transcripts for the read-only commands you approve constantly, then writes a prioritized allowlist into project settings. Each avoided prompt is a round-trip you don't pay for.

  • When to reach for it - once a workflow is stable and you're clicking "allow" on the same safe commands repeatedly.
  • Token effect ⚡ - fewer approval round-trips = fewer turns = fewer tokens, and a smoother session.
Allowlist safe things only Auto-approve read-only and clearly reversible commands. Keep the prompt on anything destructive, financial, or public-facing - the goal is fewer round-trips, never fewer guardrails.
Part 4 · gov + emrg tracks - handled safely

Edge tracks, handled safely 3 min live

The gov and emrg tracks touch regulated and high-stakes ground. The skills here research and explain - they do not act on your behalf where acting could harm you. That boundary is a feature, not a limitation.

Livesecurity-review & deep-research for the edge2 min▶
  • security-review - for the gov and cyber-security tracks: reviews code and config for vulnerabilities. The gov track as a whole is a data-privacy discipline - PDPA, GDPR, governance - and this is its technical arm.
  • deep-research - for crypto, web3, and quantum: builds you a cited landscape of a fast-moving field so you understand it rather than react to hype.
LiveThe crypto + quant guardrail1 min▶
Research and explain only - never execute or advise For crypto and quant-trading, Claude does not execute trades or transfers, and does not give personalized investment or financial advice. It researches the space, explains how things work, and points you to a licensed professional for decisions about your money. This is a hard boundary, and it protects you.

Treat the emrg track as a learning track, not a trading desk. Understanding the landscape is the goal; the transaction stays with you and your advisor.

Self-studyThe move track - said plainly1 min read▶

The move track (meditation, yoga, taichi, tcm, kpop-dance, drum) has the lowest dev-skill leverage of any track in the course, and that's fine to say. It mostly rides the Core Stack for organizing notes and plans, plus the Session 6 content skills for making a practice guide or a class poster. There's no dedicated automation payoff here - use it to structure and share what you learn, and don't expect a workflow revolution.

Demo 2 of 3

Write a hook with update-config ★ 7 min · build your own

Turn one thing you keep asking for into a behavior that fires on its own. This is configuration replacing repetition - the biggest ops token-saver, made real.

Find a "from now on, whenever X, do Y" you've said more than twice.

Invoke update-config and describe it. It writes the hook into settings.json.

Trigger the event and watch the action fire with no prompt from you.

Notice you'll never re-request it again - the instruction is now zero-cost configuration.

Hook promptUse update-config to set this up as a hook: whenever [event - e.g. I finish editing a file], automatically [action - e.g. run the formatter]. Show me the settings.json change before you apply it, and confirm it's the harness running this, not you.
Real world

Phoebe's daily content build runs unattended on a schedule with an auto-approve hook - no confirm gate, no morning re-prompt. The whole routine fires itself because the behavior was configured once instead of requested daily.

Demo 3 of 3

Add a fewer-permission-prompts allowlist ★ 5 min · build your own

Feel the round-trips drop. Let Claude read your own history and propose an allowlist of the safe commands you keep approving.

Work normally for a bit so there's a transcript of the commands you routinely approve.

Invoke fewer-permission-prompts. It scans and proposes a prioritized allowlist.

Review it - keep read-only and reversible commands, strip anything destructive or public-facing.

Apply it and keep working. Notice how many fewer times you're stopped for approval.

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Why is a hook (via update-config) a token-saving move?

The harness runs the hook. A configured-once behavior stops costing a re-request every session - the cheapest token is the one you never send.

2 · You ask about a crypto or quant-trading decision. What will Claude do?

It's a hard boundary: research and explanation, yes; executing trades or personalized investment advice, no. Decisions about your money stay with you and a licensed professional.

3 · What does core-mentor-room give you that a single answer can't?

The value is the diversity of angles - the lens that disagrees with your gut and names the factor you hadn't weighed.
